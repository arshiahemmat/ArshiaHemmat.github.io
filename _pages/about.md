## Introduction

I’m a **researcher at the University of Oxford** and a computer engineering graduate exploring how **multimodal large language models** can be made more reliable, interpretable and aligned with human values.  My work bridges computer vision, natural language processing and machine learning, with a particular emphasis on **trustworthy AI**, **generative models**, and **zero‑shot learning**.  Drawing on experiences across academia and industry, I aim to build systems that can reason about the world through both visual and textual information while remaining robust under distribution shift and adversarial pressure.

## Research Interests

My research sits at the intersection of perception, language and reasoning.  Current topics include:

- **Multimodal LLMs and retrieval‑augmented generation:** designing systems that can answer questions about images or video by combining generative models with external knowledge and uncertainty estimation.
- **Trustworthy and interpretable AI:** auditing failure modes in vision‑language models, developing techniques for uncertainty calibration and privacy preservation, and coupling generative models with governance layers.
- **Generative and diffusion models:** using diffusion and geometric deep learning to create datasets and explore how training data influences model behaviour.
- **Zero‑shot and few‑shot learning:** enabling models to recognise unseen concepts, particularly in the medical and educational domains.

## Selected Publications

Here are a few recent works.  A complete list is available on the **[Publications](/publications/)** page and my [Google Scholar]({{ author.googlescholar }}).

- **[Hidden in Plain Sight: Evaluating Abstract Shape Recognition in Vision‑Language Models](https://arshiahemmat.github.io/illusionbench/)** – *NeurIPS 2024*.  Introduces **IllusionBench**, a dataset that embeds letters, faces and animals inside everyday scenes to audit whether state‑of‑the‑art models truly perceive gestalt structure.  Humans solve these tasks near‑perfectly, whereas leading models score below 40 % zero‑shot.

- **[Research Directions for Using LLM in Software Requirement Engineering: A Systematic Review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2025.1519437/full)** – *Frontiers in Computer Science 2025*.  A comprehensive review of how large language models are being deployed in requirements engineering, from automation to stakeholder communication.

- **[Context Awareness Gate for Retrieval‑Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.16133)** – *IKT 2025*.  Proposes a mechanism that dynamically adjusts the input prompts of large language models based on the necessity of external context retrieval.

- **[Leveraging Retrieval‑Augmented Generation for Persian University Knowledge Retrieval](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10892716)** – *IEEE IKT 2024*.  Describes a two‑stage pipeline that combines Persian language models with tailored prompt engineering to answer university‑related queries.

- **[Adaptive Chunking for VideoRAG Pipelines with a Newly Gathered Bilingual Educational Dataset](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10967455)** – *CSICC 2025*.  Leverages a bilingual video dataset to propose a retrieval‑augmented video question answering pipeline with adaptive chunking.

- **[CLIP Exhibits Improved Compositional Generalization Through Representation Disentanglement](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=UVSKuh9eK5)** – *Preprint, ICLR 2024 submission*.  Investigates how compositional out‑of‑distribution generalisation of CLIP models emerges from training data diversity and representation disentanglement.

## Research Experiences

My journey as a researcher spans several institutions and projects.  For a comprehensive timeline, see the dedicated **[Research](/research/)** page.  Highlights include:
